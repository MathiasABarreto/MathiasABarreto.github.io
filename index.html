About

I develop control and optimization methods for advanced manufacturing. My PhD research, in partnership with Honeywell, targets lithium-ion battery electrode production: I combine high-fidelity particle simulations with Bayesian optimization and data-driven models to tune and control process parameters.

I am co-supervised by Dr. Philip Loewen and Dr. Bhushan Gopaluni, and am a member of the Data Analytics & Intelligent Systems Lab and the Institute of Applied Mathematics.

Before UBC, I completed my MSc in Mathematics at HSE University in Moscow, Russia with Dr. Harold Moreno. There I worked on the stochastic version of the bin packing problem, supported by Huawei's Mathematical Modelling Lab. I hold a BSc in Economics from the Catholic University of Asunción.

Research

PhD · Honeywell collaboration

Particle-scale (DEM) modelling of electrode calendering

I simulate the calendering of NMC cathodes, starting from particle arrangements reconstructed from X-ray CT scans. Using the Discrete Element Method, I compare contact force models and aim to reproduce the mechanical and electrochemical behavior of the compressed electrode. My current focus is on modelling the carbon-binder domain (CBD), relaxing the spherical assumption of active material, and making the simulations faster.

Publications

Optimal sub-Gaussian variance proxy for truncated Gaussian and exponential random variables
M. Barreto, O. Marchal, J. Arbel
Statistics & Probability Letters, 2025

We find, in closed form, the smallest sub-Gaussian variance proxy of truncated Gaussian and truncated exponential random variables. A truncated Gaussian is strictly sub-Gaussian (its optimal proxy equals its variance) exactly when the truncation is symmetric about the mean; a truncated exponential never is.

Earlier work

MSc 2023 · HSE University · Huawei Mathematical Modelling Lab

Stochastic bin packing under chance constraints

In this setting, items with random sizes must be packed into as few bins as possible, while each bin may overflow only with a small, set probability. For sizes that follow a sub-Gaussian distribution, I replaced this probabilistic constraint with a simple rule based on the items' moments (a Chernoff bound) and proved that it performs as well as the original problem as the number of items grows. I also give a geometric argument to compute the optimal variance proxy of a truncated normal distribution.
